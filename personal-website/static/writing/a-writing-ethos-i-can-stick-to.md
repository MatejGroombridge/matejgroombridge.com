Every time I have tried to "start a blog" I have collapsed under the weight of imagined rules: a posting schedule, a niche, an audience, a voice. None of those are wrong, but together they are heavier than the writing itself. So I am writing down a smaller set of rules I can actually live with.

## Write to think, not to publish

The first draft exists to clarify my own mind. If a piece never makes it past that, the time was still well spent. Publishing is a bonus, not the point.

## Short by default

A short essay that is finished beats a long essay that is permanently almost-ready. If something genuinely needs more room, it will earn it.

## One idea per piece

If I notice a second idea trying to elbow in, it becomes its own draft. Pages with one clear claim are easier to write, easier to read, and easier to return to later.

## Plain words

No throat-clearing, no hedging, no borrowed jargon. If a sentence would embarrass me read aloud, it gets cut.

## No metrics

I am not going to look at view counts, time on page, or whatever else the analytics dashboard wants to show me. The feedback loop I care about is whether the writing helped me think more clearly, and whether the occasional reader sends a thoughtful reply.

That is the whole ethos. Small enough to remember, strict enough to bite.
