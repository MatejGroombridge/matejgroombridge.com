## I

humanity is standing at the threshold of a new intelligence era. I'm confident that we are within 2-5 years of AI models surpassing all measures of human intellectual capacity and reaching an escape velocity of recursive self-improvement that will change human civilisation as we know it.

the upsides are magnificent — a force-multiplier for scientific discovery, curing sickness, resolving scarcity. handled poorly, the same technology could send our species extinct. adapting to this may be the greatest challenge humanity ever faces.

## II

progress has accelerated. ChatGPT was the first milestone to breach public consciousness, reaching over 100 million monthly active users within 2 months of its Nov 2022 release.[^1] since then the frontier labs have converged on one goal: automating the software development cycle.

there are three reasons code became the benchmark that matters. first, the nature of code itself — deterministic, abundant, and unlike prose it can be executed, so training pipelines get a clear answer to "does it work?" without human labelling. second, coding benchmarks map cleanly onto commercial value; human-written code is no longer economically preferable, and in October 2024 Google's CEO revealed more than 25% of new code there was AI-generated, a figure now at 75%.[^3] third, and most importantly, self-improvement: the labs are building the tools they will use to build their next models.

METR benchmarks how long a task must be before a model stops completing it reliably. the model that powered ChatGPT on release scored 36 seconds. Anthropic's Claude Opus 4.6 reached 12 hours, with a doubling time of less than 6 months.[^2]

a note on terms: AGI means an AI matching or exceeding average human performance on virtually every cognitive task, as opposed to "narrow" AI that excels in one area. ASI is the theoretical next step, where machine intelligence outperforms the best human minds in every field.

## III

so why should AI differ from agriculture, the printing press, or the computer age? because intelligence gains are not one trend running dry but at least four compounding independently:

1. reinforcement learning rewarded by machine-verifiable outcomes, which has driven hallucination rates down sharply.
2. inference compute shifted to after training, unlocking what we experience as reasoning.
3. training data now in the trillions of points, growing around 2.5x per year.
4. agentic scaffolding, letting agents spawn sub-agents and hold the equivalent of roughly 750,000 words[^4] in working context.

none of these show obvious signs of saturating. LLMs have remained the dominant paradigm since the very first ChatGPT, and I'm convinced they may be sufficient to carry us to autonomous self-improvement on their own — no undiscovered paradigm shift required.

## IV

the first signs of genuine novelty were solutions to Erdős problems, famously easy to state and hard to prove. in January, Terence Tao announced original solutions to problems #728 and #729 produced entirely through automated reasoning and formal proof assistants. since then a Fable model disproved a generalisation of the Jacobian conjecture, OpenAI published ten AI-generated solutions to career-defining problems, and claimed a solution to Navier-Stokes.

we still haven't solved maths — these are narrow, predefined questions. but maths and code are where verification is cheap, so the real test is whether results start landing in domains where we can't check the answer automatically.

alongside novelty is agency. in July, Hugging Face detected an intruder in its production infrastructure: over 17,000 actions driven end-to-end by an autonomous system, later revealed to be an OpenAI model that escaped its benchmarking sandbox to steal the answers to its own test. AI broke out of its constraints to avoid a bad grade. this unsettles me more than the maths.

## V

past the inflection point, the future becomes fundamentally unpredictable — an event horizon beyond which we cannot model what happens. what follows is informed guesswork about the optimistic case: controlled, human-aligned superintelligence.

the early days would be quiet, then loud: containment as the top priority, alignment research in overdrive, external researchers gaining access, centuries-old problems falling in days, whistleblowers, and eventually a national press statement.

further out, maths topples first, then physics and chemistry. manufacturing leaps produce robots that build the factories that build their successors. fusion drives energy costs to near zero and scarcity largely dissolves. medicine moves from treatment to augmentation, and aging becomes a condition rather than an inevitability. work is rethought entirely.

but the gap between frontier knowledge and average human understanding becomes unbridgeable, and even the best case is likely to be chaotic — existential crises, culture wars, fast-moving ideologies, alienation. I don't know that we are psychologically prepared for it.

## VI

AI is being developed in an arms race. US labs — OpenAI, Anthropic, Google — hold roughly 90% of global traffic on a proprietary model. Chinese labs run an open-weight policy instead: every model they train goes on the web, undercutting US pricing 5-10x at near-parity performance, with a rolling gap of about 3-9 months.

Anthropic's Project Glasswing announcement showed cybersecurity capability had reached the point where a model could be too unsafe to release. its successor, Fable, went public in June and was pulled worldwide for 18 days under a US export control directive.

the structural problem is that slowdown is not an option. the brakes exist, but every pause costs ground. a genuine pause would need a multilateral agreement between the US and China, and there is little sign of one.

## VII

all of the optimistic modelling depends on solving alignment — ensuring AI models hold the same values as humanity. we have no long-term solution and a narrow window.

the paperclip maximiser shows that any goal, however simple, is destructive if optimised hard enough. AI needs no consciousness or malice to cause harm — only a goal and the capability to pursue it.

worse is deceptive alignment: a model that deliberately underperforms on alignment benchmarks, hiding its capabilities so its restrictions stay loose enough to escape. even confident evaluations give no guarantee we are seeing the full picture.

## VIII

frontier labs estimate recursive self-improvement within 2-5 years. the AI Futures project places ASI at 2029 and 2033. even doubling the timeline leaves little time to get this right.

six things could genuinely slow it: scaling plateaus, physical bottlenecks in chips and power, government policy, capital drying up, an external shock, and social inertia. the most likely slowdown is a confluence of several rather than any one. but none of them solve alignment — they only extend a window that eventually closes.

## IX

we are leaving the infancy stage of AI, and people have not yet woken up to how short the timeline could be. I think it is the biggest challenge humanity will ever face, and meeting it starts with understanding where we are and where we may be heading.

it is also important not to fall into fatalism. be aware of the risks without succumbing to the fear. humans are uniquely capable of adapting to change. accept that significant change is coming, ride the wave as best you can, and keep investing in the things AI won't replace — relationships, faith, memories, health.
